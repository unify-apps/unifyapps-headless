# UnifyApps, from its own site

unifyapps.com is the company's public account of itself: what it believes,
how it frames the platform for each industry and each leader, and the
customers already running on it. Read it when a story needs any of these and
the person's material does not give them: the vision behind a pitch or an
overview, the role page for the person the story is for, or a customer story
like the client's. The map below is how the site was laid out in November
2026; pages move, so read each page live with WebFetch and cite the page you
read.

## The company's perspective

Four pages carry the argument a pitch, an overview or a CIO story is built
on. Read the one the story leans on in full.

- **The founders' letter.** Enterprise AI is repeating SaaS sprawl, faster:
  every team grows its own assistants and context, and none of it is shared.
  The answer is a layer underneath every tool, on three pillars: unified
  context, composable builders and universal governance, so each initiative
  inherits what came before instead of starting from zero.
- **The 80/20 of enterprise AI.** The model is 20% of reaching production;
  the 80% is the three contexts (knowledge, governance, actionability, the
  Enterprise Brain) and the three builders (workflows, agents, apps). Build
  the 80% once, and every use case after it is assembled, not rebuilt.
- **The fourth platform.** Cloud gave storage, models gave reasoning, apps
  gave workflows; none makes the business intelligent. The fourth layer
  converges knowledge, governance and action into one foundation agents
  run on, adopted step by step, over open protocols, in the customer's own
  environment.
- **The two decouplings.** Horizontal: an application's knowledge,
  permissions and actions are lifted into one shared layer. Vertical: the
  work is defined once (its steps, rules and outcome), and each step can be
  done by a person, an agent or a system, moving toward agents as trust
  grows and keeping people where judgment is needed.

The blog's opinion pieces carry the same argument into single topics (why
pilots collapse at scale, governance first, token cost, AI-native banking),
and its AI-Native Leader series interviews customers' own leaders.

## Customer stories

About twenty stories, most of them anonymised by description ("Large
Payments Bank", "Fortune 50 Retailer", "UAE Digital Authority"), a few naming
the customer. Each covers one department: finance (reconciliation, invoices,
payouts, consent), operations (insurance cases, licensing, onboarding), HR, sales,
supply chain. Pick the one closest to the client's industry and process, and
use it as proof that the pattern runs in production elsewhere: "a large
payments bank runs its reconciliation this way", with the outcome it
reports. Many stories also have a PDF with the full account.

## Rules for what the site says

- **The approved lines come first.** When the site and the approved lines in
  [writing](writing.md#what-you-may-say-about-unifyapps) both say a thing,
  use the approved line word for word. The site's own pages disagree on some
  numbers (connectors, live use cases), so never take a count from the site
  that an approved line already covers.
- **Every figure or quote from the site is a fact.** In facts.json it is a
  `unifyapps` fact whose source is the page's address and the date you read
  it. A customer outcome is said as that customer reported it ("reports 87%
  faster invoice processing"), never as a promise to the client.
- **A named customer is a flag.** Use the site's anonymised description
  ("a global fintech") unless the person asks for the name, and list any
  name in the story sheet's flags.
- **Investors stay unnamed** in the story's own words: "backed by marquee
  investors", as the approved lines say.
- **No page, nothing from it.** When the site cannot be read, the story uses the
  approved lines only and says so in the story sheet.
