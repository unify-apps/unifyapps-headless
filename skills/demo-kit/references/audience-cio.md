# Writing for a CIO

Read this when the story is for a CIO or an IT leader (VP IT, head of
enterprise applications, chief digital officer). It sets what they answer
for, what they need to see, and the shape of a film that holds them. The
numbers in the example lines are the shape of the claim; every figure in a
real story still needs its fact and source.

## What they answer for

- **Making the AI mandate real in every function.** Finance, operations, HR
  and sales all come with lists; the CIO owns delivering all of them.
- **Three questions the CFO doesn't get asked:** can we run it? Can we
  secure it? Can we do it again, for every function?
- **The board's question:** what do we have to show? Agents in production,
  not pilots.
- **The CISO's questions:** how many agents do we actually run? Whose
  permissions do they act with? Can you show everything one of them did last
  Tuesday?
- **Sprawl and shadow AI.** Teams building their own agents outside IT, on a
  credit card. Every new stack is one more thing to run, secure and staff.

## The story they live

1. The mandate from the CEO to the board, and it falls to them.
2. Every leader arrives with a list.
3. The call that felt fastest: let every team pick its tools.
4. Month three: pilots everywhere, every demo worked.
5. Month six: one stack per pilot; every team rebuilding the same connector,
   search and guardrails; agents found later, built outside IT.
6. Wired by hand: hundreds of applications, few talking to each other, every
   integration breaking for its own reason.
7. Month twelve: three versions of the truth; agents disagreeing about the
   same customer or employee.
8. The CISO's questions nobody can answer.
9. Month eighteen: the board asks; many pilots, few in production; the bill;
   agent sprawl.

Then the turn: not the models. The 20% and the 80%, rebuilt for every agent
in every function, every layer an attack surface they answer for, and the
glue code as the lock-in.

## What they need to see

| Beat | Show | Scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lists | each function's requests landing on one desk | hub, points |
| Pilots everywhere | demos multiplying, one stack each | stats, hub `tangle` |
| Wired by hand | the same systems wired again and again, and the break reasons | hub `tangle`, points |
| Three versions of the truth | two agents, two records for one customer | before-after |
| The CISO's questions | unanswered, then answered | points |
| Pilots against production | the count the board sees | stats |
| One platform | systems, three contexts built once, one brain, builders, governance around it | platform, hub `connect` |
| One registry | every agent, its owner and its permissions | screen, points |
| One request across the organisation | a new joiner: HR, identity, devices, finance access, approval in chat | flow, screen |
| Reuse across functions | IT service desk, then HR onboarding, then the finance close: about 8, 5, 4 weeks | timeline |

Show the estate as they see it: many systems, many teams, one place where
every agent is registered and governed. Show permissions as "acts with the
permissions of the person it serves", never a service account that sees
everything.

## Their hero example

One request that crosses the whole organisation: a new joiner. HR hires her;
the onboarding agent takes over; the context graph knows her role, team and
manager; policy decides what she gets; access to finance data needs her
manager's approval in chat with the reason attached; connectors create the
accounts, order the laptop and grant the access; every step lands in the
audit trail with its cost. Use their own cross-functional process when the
material names one.

## Words

- **Use:** every function, run it, secure it, do it again, pilots against
  production, stacks, agent sprawl, built outside IT, one registry, whose
  permissions, the audit trail, three versions of the truth, the board, one
  platform, one login, one tenant, a glass box.
- **Avoid:** cost-only framing (that is the CFO's film), a list of models or
  frameworks, and blaming their team: "my people were excellent; this is the
  industry pattern".

## The film's shape

A full film runs about 10 minutes, often in the CIO's own voice for the
first part; a 2 to 3 minute cut keeps the lists, the sprawl, the CISO's
questions, the platform, the new joiner and the ask.

1. The mandate, and it falls to me.
2. Everyone came to me; the three questions I get asked.
3. My call: let every team move. Freeze frame.
4. Pilots everywhere; a stack per pilot; wired by hand; three versions of
   the truth; the CISO's questions; the board asks; the bill; agent sprawl.
5. What went wrong: not the model. The 20% and the 80%.
6. Rebuilt for every agent; the lock-in I never budgeted for.
7. The way out: assembly-first. One platform, three contexts, every team on
   one brain, one registry.
8. The eighteen months again, on UnifyApps; the new joiner, end to end.
9. The CISO's questions answered; the board's question answered.
10. Every function reuses the last; every agent makes the next one cheaper.
11. Who UnifyApps is (the approved credentials only).
12. Back to the freeze frame, and one question: "Which request on your list
    do you want in production first?"
