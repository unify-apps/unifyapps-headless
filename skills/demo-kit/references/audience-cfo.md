# Writing for a CFO

Read this when the story is for a CFO or a finance leader (VP Finance,
controller, head of accounts payable or finance operations). It sets what
they answer for, what they need to see, and the shape of a film that holds
them. The numbers in the example lines are the shape of the claim; every
figure in a real story still needs its fact and source.

## What they answer for

- **A cost target with their name on it.** The AI mandate reaches them as a
  number and a date. Every process on the whiteboard is a cost line.
- **One question above all: what does it save?** And the follow-ups: what
  does it cost to build, to run every year, to change, to replace a part?
- **The business case.** Spend they can see, agent by agent; nothing paid
  for twice; a return that grows rather than a bill that grows.
- **The auditors.** Who approved a payment, the agent or a person? Where are
  the credentials that let it post to the ERP? Can every action at
  quarter-end be shown?
- **Payment risk.** Paying the wrong amount, a duplicate payment, a vendor
  with three codes.

## The story they live

Told in their terms, the familiar arc is:

1. The mandate: AI-first, and a cost target with a date.
2. The call they signed off: build it ourselves, one team, a modest budget.
3. A demo on clean samples that worked, and phase two approved.
4. Real documents arrive in every format; the agent guesses; people check
   every output, so the planned savings go into checking.
5. Every system wired by hand, each integration a line on the bill, each
   break another change request and another invoice from the integrator.
6. Read is not matched: invoice, purchase order and goods receipt disagree.
7. The auditors' questions nobody can answer.
8. Month eighteen: still a pilot, a bill many times the budget, the cost
   target untouched. The next team starts again from scratch.

Then the turn: the model was never the problem; it is 20% of the work, and
the 80% around it (knowledge, governance, actionability, workflows, apps,
agents) is where the budget went, rebuilt for every use case, with glue code
as the lock-in no business case showed.

## What they need to see

| Beat | Show | Scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The mandate | the cost target as a number with a date | statement, stats |
| The whiteboard | processes, each with its cost line | points |
| The bill | licence, specialist, integrator, security review, stacking up | stats, timeline |
| Where it went | months against a plan that said two | timeline |
| The 80% | the layers the budget paid for, rebuilt each time | platform |
| The way out | build the 80% once; every agent assembles from it | platform, hub `connect` |
| One hard case | a messy document read, matched, routed for approval, posted | flow, screen |
| Their questions | named approver, governed credentials, the audit trail | points |
| The payback | each use case reusing the last: about 8, 5, 4, then 2 weeks | timeline, stats |
| Two endings | the same mandate, two month-eighteens | before-after |

Show money as money: a cost line, spend per agent, a bill against a budget, a
curve where each agent costs less than the last. Show the audit trail with
the cost of each step. A CFO trusts a figure only with its source: put
research figures on screen with the publisher and date.

## Their hero example

The hard invoice, end to end: handwritten, scanned, sent through a portal.
The agent reads every field with a confidence score; the context graph
resolves the vendor's code to one golden record; the three-way match finds
it over the order and outside tolerance, so approval goes to finance in chat
with the reason attached; the connector posts it to the ERP; every step lands
in the audit trail with its cost. Use their own document type when the
material names one (an insurance form, a contract, an expense report).

## Words

- **Use:** cost line, cost target, business case, budget, the bill, spend
  you can see, run cost, signed off, the auditors, a named approver, the
  audit trail, quarter-end, tolerance, straight-through processing, paid for
  twice, compounds.
- **Avoid:** engineering detail (frameworks, vector stores, models by name),
  hype words, and any saving without its source. Say a plan is suggested;
  never promise a saving.

## The film's shape

A full film runs about 10 to 12 minutes; a 2 to 3 minute cut keeps the
mandate, the bill, the 80%, the hard example, the payback and the ask.

1. The mandate, and the cost target with their name next to it.
2. The call: build it ourselves.
3. Freeze frame, jump eighteen months.
4. Demo day on samples; the real documents; wired by hand; read is not
   matched; the auditors' questions; the plan against the months; the bill.
5. What went wrong: not the model. The 20% and the 80%.
6. Code-first rebuilds the 80% every time; the lock-in is the glue code.
7. The way out: assembly-first, and the platform built for it.
8. The same project again, every failure handled; the hard example.
9. The auditors' questions answered; live at week eight.
10. Every use case reuses the last, and costs less.
11. Who UnifyApps is (the approved credentials only).
12. Back to the freeze frame: same mandate, two films.
13. How we'd start, and one question: "Which cost line do you want to take
    on first?"
